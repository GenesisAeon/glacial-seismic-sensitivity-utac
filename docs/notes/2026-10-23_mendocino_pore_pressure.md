# Discussion note: pore pressure as an inhibiting mechanism (Mendocino 2024) – relevance for H2/C2

Date: 2026-10-23. Prepared by Aeon (scout), checked by Claude. **Discussion note only, no new
analysis; does not change any frozen text or test.**

## Source

Shelly & Materna, "Cessation of Nearby Low-Frequency Earthquake Activity Following the December
2024 Mw 7.0 Mendocino Earthquake: The Likely Role of Pore Pressure Reductions", Geophysical
Research Letters (2026), https://doi.org/10.1029/2026GL126322 (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). Check depth:
abstract only (Crossref).

Verbatim from the abstract:
- "These sources usually exhibit nearly daily activity but were shut off for ∼7–25 days following
  the December 2024 moment magnitude (Mw) 7.0 Mendocino fault earthquake." (27 LFE families at the
  southern end of Cascadia)
- "Based on modeling, we infer that this cessation was not caused by induced shear stress, which
  was slightly encouraging of slip, but rather by combined clamping normal stress and reduced pore
  pressure."
- "This mechanism, which isn't captured in the common effective friction simplification, may have
  strengthened the fault and inhibited creep, without requiring fluid outflow."

## Relevance for P133

- Glacial unloading changes **both** the normal stress and the pore pressure on faults. With the
  effective-friction simplification alone, the Coulomb promotion of unloading may be
  **overestimated**.
- **Possible counter-mechanism, and a lag.** Unloading lowers the normal stress (unclamping). Under
  undrained conditions, the pore pressure falls with the mean stress (Skempton), which **counters**
  the promotion. Only after drainage, on the poroelastic diffusion time scale, does the unloading act
  fully. A glacier-related signal could therefore appear **delayed** rather than immediately.
  `docs/LAG_MODEL.md` treats pore pressure as an immediate channel (melt-water pore pressure,
  section on measurable channels). This note adds the opposite, undrained sign.
- For C2 (prospective, background-rate change 2027–2036) this is a reason why a null result does
  not rule out a delayed effect. It is not a reason to change the test.

## Limitations

- A single event, a coseismic transient (days), and LFEs and creep rather than regular earthquakes.
- Transfer to slow glacial unloading (years to millennia, largely drained) is open.
- Abstract only; the model details (poroelastic parameters, geometry) were not read.
